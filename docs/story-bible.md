# UNDERDOG CITY — STORY BIBLE

> Canon reference for the novel, the anime, the music's narrative layer, and the
> lore drops. Extends Section 5 of the master brief; where this doc and the brief
> agree, the brief stands; where this doc deepens or resolves an open question, this
> is canon. Tone, world, and the two magic systems are locked here.

---

## 1. Premise · Thesis · Promise

**Premise.** Above the world hangs **the Halo**, a ring of light where the
**Halo-born** decide who has worth. Below is **Underdog City**, the sprawl where
the worthless are thrown. The most thrown-away person in it died at the bottom,
came back wrong, and is building a throne out of everything the world discarded.

**Thesis.** *The world throws people away. Down here, the broken discover that
broken things hold the most power — and build a kingdom out of everything that
was discarded.*

**Audience promise.** Make the misfits and outcasts lovable, relatable, and
powerful. Give the discarded a home and a crown. The hard shell hides a
found-family heart.

**The central irony the whole IP runs on:** *salvation is the real horror.* The
thing that calls itself mercy is the machine eating everyone.

---

## 2. Themes

- **Your damage is your blueprint, not your shame.** What broke you is what you're
  made of and what you fight with.
- **Worth is not granted from above; it's claimed from below.** "We all rule down
  here."
- **Acceptance vs. consumption.** You can wear your fracture as a crown, or be
  hollowed by it. The Halo offers a third lie — let *them* take it from you and
  call it heaven.
- **Creation from damage vs. perfection through consumption.** The broken make
  meaning out of pain; the Halo-born can only take what the broken make. (Encoded
  in the two magics: Gild vs Gilt.)
- **Becoming the monster on purpose** — as armor, as weapon, as refusal to beg the
  world for a better story.

---

## 3. The World — the vertical is the moral axis

Up = clean, radiant, empty, "saved." Down = broken, grimy, alive, real. Every
fall is a fall *into* power the system doesn't understand it's creating.

- **THE HALO.** The gleaming elite ring above. Not a city of people so much as an
  **apparatus**: the Halo-born at the top, the Sainted husks as its visible
  angels/enforcers. Cold, self-righteous, sells redemption as a product.
- **THE DROP.** The sentence. When the Halo rules you worthless, you fall. An
  execution dressed as mercy — *"we're letting you go."*
- **UNDERDOG CITY.** The sprawl where the Dropped land. Neon and garbage, surreal
  and grimy, run by misfit districts. The deeper down, the more thrown-away — and,
  secretly, the more **gilded**, because that's where the most broken are. Tone
  blend: *Gachiakuta × Edgerunners × Dorohedoro × Vox Channel-of-heart.*

**The buried truth of the world:** Underdog City is not a slum the Halo neglects.
It is a **farm.** Suffering is the crop. (See §7.)

---

## 4. The Three Castes

1. **The Dropped (the gilded).** The broken below. They **gild** (§5). Us. The
   protagonist's people.
2. **The Sainted.** Hollowed gilded raised back up as radiant husks — the visible
   "angels" and enforcers of the Halo. What the city *thinks* rules above. They
   don't. They're tools: last season's harvest, walking.
3. **The Halo-born.** *Native* to the Halo. Never fell, never broke, never gilded.
   They are the "something bigger" behind the Saints — the apex. They wield
   **Gilt** (§6) and they **consume the broken to exist** (§7). Almost no one below
   knows this caste is even real.

The city fears the Saints. The Saints are just harvested neighbors. The real
thing was never broken at all.

---

## 5. Magic I — GILD (the power of the broken)

Gold that fills a **real fracture**: structural, load-bearing, earned. *Creation
from damage.*

- **The Break is specific.** You don't gild from a hard life. You gild from a
  single nameable wound — the precise thing that broke you. **The power is always
  a literal metaphor for the wound.** Your damage is your blueprint.
- **The trigger.** Hit absolute bottom + *survive the break* → gold runs through
  the fracture → you gild.
- **The Gleam (the ledger).** Gold is visible and spendable. Every use spreads it
  as glowing veins across the skin — hands first (early), throat and eyes last
  (near the end). You can read someone's danger off their body. A blazing gilded
  is either powerful or about to die, and you can't tell which from outside.
- **On-wound is cheap; off-wound is ruinous.** Using your gild in line with your
  wound costs little. Forcing it to do what it wasn't broken to do burns gold
  fast. **Mastery = understanding your own damage precisely**, not getting
  "stronger."
- **Re-breaking (growth costs suffering).** Gold is not one-life-finite. A new,
  deep enough wound lets you gild *again* — a second power linked to the new
  break. But every fracture you carry **raises your shatter risk.** Getting
  stronger means choosing to be hurt worse.
- **The Crown (acceptance).** Those who *accept* their fracture — own the wound,
  stop fighting the gold — set it into a fixed, jagged diadem instead of letting it
  spread wild. Acceptance doesn't end the cost; it grants **control** and slows the
  spread. Building "a throne at the bottom" is this at civilization scale.
- **Terminal states (gold reaches the heart):**
  - **Shatter** — you die. Clean, final, *yours.*
  - **Hollow → Ascend** — you don't die. The gold finishes filling you, scoops out
    who you were, and you rise — calm, radiant, empty — to be reclaimed as one of
    the **Sainted.** This is the horror inversion: heaven is what's left when
    everything that made you *you* has been burned away.

---

## 6. Magic II — GILT (the power of the Halo-born)

Gold *leaf* — a thin shine laid over an unbroken, hollow surface. Decorative,
applied, borrowed. *Perfection through consumption.* The exact mirror of Gild.

- **They cannot gild.** No wounds, no fractures, nothing to fill. The Halo-born are
  creatively empty by nature.
- **Their only innate power is the Claim** — the ability to **take.** Everything
  else they wield is stolen.
- **Harvested gold = borrowed power.** Hollowing a gilded strips the gold out of
  them (the empty husk left behind becomes a **Sainted**). Wearing that gold, a
  Halo-born wields the dead person's gild — with **no Gleam, no shatter risk, no
  cost** — because the cost was already paid, by the person they emptied.
- **A wardrobe of stolen miracles.** A Halo-born can wear many harvested gilds at
  once, radiant and seamless. Their perfection is other people's pain, worn as a
  garment.
- **100% dependent.** They generate nothing. Starve the harvest and the Halo-born
  **wither** — costless power was always borrowed time. Their superiority is a lie:
  the master caste's sole talent is appropriation.

**The horror in one line:** *the gilded pay for power with their own bodies; the
Halo-born pay with someone else's.*

---

## 7. The Closed Loop (the farm)

> Drop the "worthless" → they hit bottom → they **gild** → their suffering becomes
> gold → the Halo harvests and **hollows** them (a Saint-husk left as an enforcer)
> → the gold is carried up → the Halo-born wear it as **Gilt** → which sustains and
> powers them → so they need *more* broken → so they keep **Dropping** people.

The economy is the antagonist. The war is economic at its root: **feed the
machine, or starve it.** The protagonist — king of the discarded, *protection not
conquest* — is the one who starts cutting the supply line.

**The secret that breaks the economy (§ hidden canon — no one in-world knows):**
hollowing is **reversible.** It un-does by *giving the gold back.* Every Halo-born
is unknowingly wearing the keys to revive the people they emptied. The day a
character learns stolen Gilt can be **returned**, the entire harvest becomes a
hostage crisis. Pure plot fuel; the characters grieve people who could come back.

---

## 8. The Sainted (the husks)

Radiant, calm, empty. Former gilded, hollowed, their gold harvested upward, raised
back as the Halo's angels and enforcers. They believe — genuinely, serenely —
that they were *saved*, and that they're saving everyone they come to collect.
They are the most tragic figures in the world: every Saint is a neighbor who lost.
(And, per §7, every Saint is — secretly — recoverable.)

---

## 9. The Protagonist (the masked MC)

Reference DNA: *Jinwoo × Benimaru.* The most thrown-away person in the city.
Dropped, **died at the bottom, came back wrong.**

- **His wound = being thrown away itself.** So his gild is the widest possible
  expression of it: **he can call the discarded** — forgotten people, cast-off
  objects, the unmourned dead, anything the city decided was garbage. **A
  shadow-army that grows every time the Halo throws something away.** The Halo's
  entire economy of disposal is secretly feeding the king of the discarded.
- **Cost:** the Gleam, like everyone (§5). His power is paid in his own gold and
  his own shatter-risk — *not* in memory (see below).
- **The amnesia is a one-time scar, not a running tax.** Coming back wrong cost him
  his memory *once.* He returned with holes — doesn't know his name, X's face, who
  killed him, or the promise he made. He hunts the same truth as the audience; the
  full reveal lands on him and us together at the Season 1 finale.
- **Drive: protection, not conquest.** He builds a throne at the bottom to give the
  unwanted a home and a crown — the landlord-king of misfits (*"Now Accepting
  Tenants"*).
- **Masked.** Face hidden/changed. The artist persona = this character. The
  eventual unmasking is both a story climax and a real-world brand event.

## 10. The Love Interest

- **Halo-born, and cracking from the inside.** Born to the caste that feeds on the
  world below.
- **She does not gild** — she never hit the bottom; she's outside the kintsugi
  economy entirely. And she **has not been "fitted" with Gilt** — never wore a
  harvest, or refuses to — which is why she neither gilds nor wields. Her power is
  **birthright**: access, legitimacy, and the slow, dangerous dawn of the truth
  about what her own people are.
- Handed the **villain story** about the MC. Drawn to him; pulling the one thread
  that doesn't fit. **Hunts the truth from the opposite direction as the
  audience.**
- **Her arc:** refusing her inheritance once she understands her radiance would be
  a dead neighbor. A daughter of the harvesters, falling for the king of the
  harvested.
- **Unmasking scene (Season 1 cliffhanger).** He removes the mask not as proof of
  innocence but as **trust with no safety net** — *"I swore I'd never be the monster
  you could see."* He doesn't know if the vow was made to *her.* Open question: does
  she hold the memory he's missing — or take what she learns back to the Halo?

---

## 11. Antagonists

- **The hero-killer (immediate).** The man with the very clean boots. Halo-protected,
  framed as the hero who "put the monster down," handed a medal for the murder. The
  Halo laundered a murder into heroism and a hero into a monster. The MC's most
  personal target.
- **The Halo-born (structural).** The apex caste; the "something bigger" behind the
  Saints. Parasites in radiant clothing. They are the *reason* for the farm.
- **Beautiful Narcosis (factional).** A worship-oblivion cult that has fallen in
  love with the high of being consumed — devotees who *want* to be hollowed,
  toxic-devotion-as-religion. The album of the same name is their gospel.
- **The deepest mystery (long arc).** *Why* does breaking make gold? *Why* can the
  Halo-born only take? Where did the Claim come from? The origin of the whole
  system is the series' final unanswered question — seeded, not yet answered.

## 12. The Central Mystery & Fair-Play Clues

Locked cold open (Episode 1): audience sees **feet/ankles, a shadow slumping into
arms, blood dripping.** Reads as: a killer standing over a body he caused.

**Truth:** the body *is* the MC. He died trying to protect **X** and **failed.**
The real killer (Halo-protected) is **framed as a hero and celebrated**; the dead
outcast is **branded the villain.** The MC **gilds by dying**, comes back into a
world that already calls him the monster — and **stops correcting it.** Becomes the
monster on purpose, as armor and weapon.

**Fair-play grammar:** every reveal is *visible but misread* the first time. The
cold open's misdirection (killer-over-victim → he IS the victim) is the show's
whole grammar — **the city misreads the monster exactly the way you misread the
opening.**

**The buried object (the Season 1 thread).** In the cold open, something of X is
physically pressed into his hand and his fingers fold shut around it. On his
return, **his hand is open and empty.** The lost object is the season's central
mystery — losing it "hurts worse than the dying did." Plant 3–4 concrete
images/objects in Episode 1 that pay off later; the object is the big one.

## 14. Season 1 Arc Skeleton (working)

Mapped to the catalog; ends on the unmasking cliffhanger. Episodes are beats, not
final counts.

1. **Came Back Wrong** — the death, the lie, the gilding. The monster opens its
   eyes in the dark. (Cold open + buried object.)
2. **Down Here** — the MC lands in Underdog City; the world and its rules; the
   creed. First "tenant" recruited.
3. **Stupid Little Bitch / Who TF** — first clashes with the Halo's Saints;
   establish gild-vs-Gilt in combat without explaining it.
4. **The House / Pillow Full of Proof** — found-family episodes; allies whose
   gilds deepen the system's rules; the missing-object thread tightens.
5. **Saints vs Sinners** — the doctrinal war; the Halo's gospel vs the undercity's
   counter-gospel; Beautiful Narcosis cult introduced.
6. **Muscle Memory / Atrophy** — the cost made visible: a defector and a
   consumed-by-the-Gleam tragedy. The audience learns what hollowing really is.
7. **Say Hello** — the MC closes on the hero-killer; the love interest crosses the
   thread from the opposite side; the truth of the cold open surfaces for them.
8. **After the Fire** — aftermath/rebuild; the throne takes shape; acceptance and
   the crown.
9. **Monster (finale)** — the unmasking. Trust with no safety net. The memory
   reveal lands on MC and audience together. Cliffhanger: does she hold his missing
   truth — or carry it back to the Halo?

## 15. Tone & Prose Craft (governs the novel)

**Voice:** *Rebecca Yarros × Dean Koontz* — first-person, present tense,
propulsive, voice-y, emotionally direct, atmospheric dread. The locked prologue is
the tuning fork; match its rhythm, its short gut-punch sentences, its dark humor
under pressure.

**Rules of the prose:**
- **Fair play.** Every twist is *visible but misread.* Plant it in plain sight,
  let the reader's assumption do the hiding. Never cheat a reveal.
- **POV discipline.** The MC's fractured memory = a genuinely limited POV; he
  can't narrate what he can't remember, and the holes are felt, not explained. The
  love interest's chapters approach the same truth from the opposite direction.
- **Show the wound, name nothing.** Power, grief, and lore are shown through
  concrete sensory detail and action, not exposition dumps. Three concrete details
  per scene minimum (one object, one action, one moment in time).
- **The cold-open grammar everywhere.** Lead the reader to the wrong read, then
  turn it — at the chapter scale and the season scale.
- **Earn the heart.** The found-family warmth is the payoff under the hard shell;
  let it land in small moments, never sentimentality.
- **Read aloud.** If a line is awkward to say, it's wrong. (Same discipline as the
  lyrics.)

## 16. Open Threads / Future Seasons

- The **origin of the system** — why breaking makes gold, why the Halo-born can
  only take, where the Claim came from. The series' deepest unanswered question.
- **Reversal of hollowing** discovered in-world — the bomb that turns the harvest
  into a hostage crisis.
- **Nightmares of Neverland** — a dream-realm layer beneath the undercity (later
  season / passion project).
